# Review, 2026-09-25

**The pipeline keeps the author's content but misstates the author's
certainty, and its misstatements concentrate in the layers you are told you
can stop at. The epub files carry mechanical damage no test catches. Nothing
measures whether any of it helps you read.**

Scope: 4 sample books (98 rewritten documents), 8 chapters audited claim by
claim and re-checked by a second pass, 58 code findings each verified by a
skeptic, 264 research citations checked (250 confirmed, 14 misstated, 0 not
found). No provider was run. Raw data is local only, in
`experiments/review-2026-09-25/` (gitignored, it holds book text):
`pairs/`, `audits/*.verified.json`, `metrics.csv`, `metrics.md`,
`research/*.md`.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The top layers overstate the author and Key points repeat it

Three high-severity inventions, all in the answer or shape layer and all
repeated in Key points:

- Stats ch4 (`statistics-done-wrong/011.opt.md:33`): "Benjamini–Hochberg
  lets the data show how many are [true]". The author says the false
  discovery rate can be computed "only by guessing" (orig:160). Standard BH
  estimates neither.
- SRE ch22 (`029.opt.md:320`): remedies listed "roughly from least to most
  drastic". The source gives no order, and its "big hammer" sits 4th of 7.
- HLW ch9 (`016.opt.md:8`): DNS is grouped with jobs "too dynamic for the
  kernel". The author's reason is that DNS is an application-layer protocol
  (orig:558). The rewrite names the application layer in parentheses, but
  the causal claim is new.

Medium examples: "guide the negotiations" became "replaces the negotiation"
(SRE ch3), "part of the reason … usually" became "because" (Rust ch2), two
"probably"s dropped from the port-number claim (HLW ch9).

**Mechanism.** Three prompt lines push the way the model already leans.
`body.md:99` ("literal and flat … hedges become calibrated claims") executes
as deletion. `body.md:93` ("write because, so…") adds links the author never
made: connectives rise from 9.5 to 16.1 per 1,000 words in 76/76 chapters.
`body.md:36` ("the mechanism that makes the answer true") invites one
mechanism where the author listed independent factors. The apparatus pass
sees only the body, so it quotes the error back as study material. External
work finds the same bias: LLM summaries overgeneralise at odds ratio 4.85
against expert summaries (Peters & Chin-Yee 2025, RSOS), and rewriters raise
certainty 1.5–2× more often than they lower it (Belem et al. 2026, 5 models).
For a reader who stores exact wording, a strengthened claim is stored as a
rule.

Error density by layer: answer 3.87, shape 2.73, depths 0.57 per 1,000
words. Answer plus shape is 17% of body words and holds 53% of located
errors (45 lines, not all re-verified, so the magnitude is approximate).

### 2. Nothing measures the goal

Each option A–O was chosen on your read of chapter 1 of 3 books, one draw
each. A 3/3 preference has sign-test p = 0.25. Felt comprehension tracks
actual comprehension at r ≈ 0.18–0.24 (Yang et al. 2022, 115 studies). The
largest randomised trial of LLM simplification found +3.9 points of
comprehension (Guidroz et al. 2025, n = 4,563), an effect invisible without
measurement. `specs/prompt.md` attributed the experiment-to-production gap
to generation variance. That was never measured, and this review's own
first-pass audits over-flagged: 99 of 218 flags were refuted on re-check.

### 3. The HTML↔markdown round trip breaks four constructs

| Construct | Where it breaks | Instance |
|---|---|---|
| Code containing a line-initial ```` ``` ```` | html→md: markdownify fences with 3 backticks | Rust ch6 from Listing 6-7: 1,499 words of prose inside `<pre>`, listings as `<p>` |
| Code nested in a list item | html→md indent 3, md→html wants 4 | HLW: 13 of 22 `li > pre` lost `<pre>` |
| Definition lists | md→html: no `def_list` extension (`convert.py:32`) | SRE `<dl>` 49 → 0, 137 literal `:   ` lines |
| Bare `<placeholder>` in model prose | md→html passes it as a tag | HLW `/run/user/<uid>` renders `/run/user/` (8 cases) |

Reproduced in isolation for the first, third and fourth. No test
round-trips a real book. Fixing the converter does not repair shipped
chapters: their cached `NNN.md` was generated from the broken input, so
about 25 chapters need paid regeneration.

### 4. Truncated and refused output ships as `rewritten`

HLW ch4 body ends at `### ⟦AN` (`011.opt.md:993`), section 4.6.3 gone, body
ratio 0.639. `sane()` (`pipeline.py:58`) has a 5% floor, so truncation
passes even measured alone. It also counts the apparatus, so a 15-word
refused body passes at 0.086. `apparatus.assemble` returns title plus body
when KEY POINTS is missing, and the chapter caches as `rewritten` with no
note. A failed apparatus call throws away the paid body (`cli.py:159`).
`--output-format text` discards the stop reason. An output cap is the
suspected cause of HLW ch4, not confirmed.

### 5. `restore_captions` deletes prose and anchors

It replaces the first unclassed `<p>` that starts with a caption label
(`blocks.py:414`). Result: 13 duplicate ids (Rust 11, HLW 2), 16 broken Rust
fragment links (index `Page_NNN` anchors sat in those paragraphs), prose
lost in Rust ch9 and HLW ch8.

### 6. The depths are the author's sections renamed

Depth paragraph order against the source: tau median 0.995, 60/76 chapters
≥ 0.9. The shape layer names 56% of depth sections. 52 confirmed
restatements across 8 chapters (Rust ch8 states "the compiler writes the
state machine" 4 times). The spec says the author's order does not survive.
It does. A structure where stopping early "loses resolution, never the
shape" has to restate at lower resolution, and `body.md:57-67` forbids
restating, so the model restates anyway. The 8-gram repetition proxy reads
0% median and misses all of this: paraphrase, not copy.

### 7. 22 of 98 rewrites are not chapters

3 copyright pages, 9 forewords, prefaces, acknowledgments and praise pages,
3 part dividers, 6 SRE appendices, and Stats "Appendix A. Notes". That is
22.4% of calls, and the documents grew (22,937 → 24,014 words). HLW's rights
notice was paraphrased. No front-matter rule exists in code despite
`specs/prompt.md`. `REFERENCE_TITLES` matches whole titles only, so
"Appendix A. Notes" fails (plain spaces fail too). The primer lists front
matter, so cumulative questions quiz the SRE Preface.

### 8. SRE titles appear twice in 43 of 45 documents

Pandoc puts the chapter id on a wrapper, so the body opens with an
⟦ANCHOR⟧ line. `apparatus._title` checks only `lines[0]`, falls back to the
source title, and leaves the body's heading in place (`apparatus.py:41-48`).

### 9. Tests pin functions, not the pipeline

The fixtures carry `=== ORIENT ===`. Making assembly key on ORIENT alone
drops every Key point, question and answer with all 157 tests green (M01).
Dropping the banned-word retry assignment ships the dirty body green (M04).
Same-file and TOC anchor collection are unpinned (M12 alone drops SRE's
protected targets 671 → 269). No fixture has the production apparatus shape.

### 10. Length overshoot is a fixed cost

Body words ≈ 612 + 0.597 × input (R² = 0.96, n = 76). The marginal rate sits
inside the band. The intercept is the answer and shape layers, so short
chapters cannot reach 70%: 1/14 chapters under 3,000 words land in band
against 12/17 at 8,000+. Sentences over 35 words doubled (8.6% → 16.1%)
against `body.md:97`. Semicolons and em dashes tripled. Whether this is a
defect is your call (decision 3): the approved prompt says comprehension
outranks the target.

### Below the cut

- Epub hygiene: `lang` dropped from 37 documents (`epub.py:113`), the ISBN

  meaningless, 37–38 "Section N.N" references dangle, curly quotes 16.2 →
  0.2 per 1,000 words.
- Failure paths: primer failure aborts the run (`cli.py:126`), usage-limit
  errors retried 3× with no circuit breaker, `--force` re-bills finished
  chapters on a recovery run.
- Latent, synthetic input only: SVG lowercased, `<mml:math>` unprotected,
  single-quoted `href` missed, repeated listings swap, non-raster cover
  crashes after all chapters are paid for.
- `epub.py:84` fuses title text without a separator ("1The Big Picture"),
  and the primer context says "chapter 8" for spine index 8.
- CLAUDE.md puts rationale in docs, but `epub.py:37-42`, `blocks.py:33-42`,
  `:107-114`, `:397-406` and `pipeline.py:11-13` carry it in comments.

## Decisions for you

1. **Change `body.md:93` and `:99`?** Recommended: yes, validated by option
   2 on 3 draws before promotion. It fixes the cause at zero token cost and
   brings the prompt back to guideline 15 (explicit uncertainty). The
   alternative keeps O byte-identical and adds the check-and-patch pass: it
   protects the prose you approved, costs 20–35% more tokens, and relies on
   verifiers that miss unfaithful claims.
2. **How to validate?** Recommended: a blind excerpt-pair test this week,
   then an ABBA crossover on your next unread book (original and optimised
   chapters alternating, cued-recall quiz 2–7 days later written from the
   original, scale items on hedged claims (always / usually / sometimes /
   rarely / not stated; true/false raises later belief in false statements,
   Arcos 2024), reading minutes
   logged). 10 chapters per arm detects only d ≈ 1.3, so run it across
   books. The alternative is the pair test alone: one evening, but it
   measures preference, which tracks learning weakly.
3. **Is 55–70% a target or a driver?** Recommended: a driver. Keep the
   override, report the body ratio per chapter, and stop counting overshoot
   as failure. The alternative is per-layer budgets (answer 1–2 paragraphs,
   shape scaled to input), which puts about 60/98 in band and edits the
   approved O structure.

## Limits

- Fidelity numbers come from 8 chapters × 40 claims, audited and re-checked
  by LLM passes of the same family as the generator (inferred). No pass
  searched for missed errors.
- Hedge, absolute and connective rates are lexical proxies. Some deleted
  hedges are filler.
- The provider, model version and effort that built the samples are
  unrecorded. Stats was rebuilt 2026-09-25, the rest 2026-09-16.
- Most learning-science samples are students or lay readers. No study tests
  this pipeline's output on any reader.
- Brann & Sidi 2025 was read through a secondary report.
